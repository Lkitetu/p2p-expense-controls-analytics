## Bottom line
Of **443,596 in-scope vouchers ($8.83B)**, the control tests flag **23,277 vouchers (5.8%) worth $367.8M** for review. Three areas deserve attention first:

1. **Duplicate-payment review: 2,708 voucher pairs ($84.2M).** Each pair matches on vendor, PO, fund, account, and amount, posted within 7 days. The largest is two **$21,646,221** vouchers to the Regents of the University of California (DPH) posted the same day, **twice** (April 2 and May 21, 2025). That's consistent with catch-up installments, but at this size the invoice schedule should be confirmed.
2. **Recurring use of "one-time" direct vouchers: 145 vendor relationships ($51.6M).** That's half of all in-scope direct-voucher spend. SF's own guidance limits direct vouchers to one-time, non-recurring payments. Vendors like Richmond Area Multi-Services (DPH, 12 months, $7.3M) and CityBase (MTA, 12 months, $4.2M) look like candidates for a PO or contract.
3. **Possible split purchases under the $20,000 delegated limit: 460 clusters ($17.4M).** These are 2–5 documents of $10K–$20K each to one vendor within 14 days, together over $20K. Splitting to stay under the limit is prohibited. DPH (133 clusters), MTA (85), and ADM (80) account for the most.

**Spending path:** 90.4% of in-scope spend was paid on contract. **$845M (9.6%)** went out without one: $459M on purchase orders with no contract and $386M by direct voucher with no PO.

**Supplier concentration:** 15 spend categories of $10M+ are highly concentrated (HHI > 2,500), covering **$956M**. Examples: Automotive & Other Vehicles (Siemens Mobility, 78% of $177M) and Pharmaceutical (McKesson, 63% of $139M). These are the strongest candidates for competitive sourcing review.

**Benford's law:** overall first-digit conformity is **close** (MAD 0.0025). Nine departments are nonconforming. The largest, Elections (MAD 0.13, digit 2 overrepresented), is most likely explained by fixed-rate payments rather than manipulation, and should be confirmed with the department.

**Period-end:** there's no citywide June spike. June 2025 payments were **22% below** the July–May average, as expected on a cash basis since June invoices are paid in July. At the department level, Recreation & Park ran **+89%** in June and is worth a look.

## Recommended next steps
1. Pull invoices for the **62 Tier A duplicate pairs of $100K+ ($64.0M)** and confirm or recover.
2. Send the 145 recurring direct-voucher relationships to department procurement leads to move onto POs or contracts.
3. Sample 25 split-purchase clusters for documentation of separate needs.
4. Add Tier A duplicate logic as a **pre-payment** check, so it stops payments instead of finding them afterward.

## Limitations
The public dataset has no invoice numbers, invoice dates, or approval records, so every flag is a lead, not a conclusion. Identical installments on fixed-fee contracts can look like duplicates. Public agencies, banks, and benefit carriers were excluded from the direct-voucher and concentration tests using a documented name rule, which may miss or include a few payees.
